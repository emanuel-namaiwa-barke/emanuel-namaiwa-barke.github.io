My academic journey

I am originally from Niger, where I attended the prestigious Lycée d'excellence de Niamey. I graduated in 2017 with a mathematics baccalaureate (mention Bien). This demanding secondary education laid the foundations for an academic journey that has taken me from Niger to Tunisia and then to France.

I pursued my university studies at the Faculty of Economics and Management of Sousse, Tunisia. I earned a bachelor's degree in quantitative economics in 2020, followed by a research master's degree in Economic Engineering and Applied Econometrics in 2022, graduating with honours. I received the faculty's Dean's Prize in 2019 and 2021. In 2021, I ranked first in my cohort and obtained the faculty's highest overall academic distinction (major des majors).

During the 2019–2020 academic year, I had the opportunity to study at Université Côte d'Azur in Nice, France, through an Erasmus+ mobility programme, at its Institute of Economics and Management (ISEM). This experience strengthened the international dimension of my training and exposed me to a different academic environment.

In November 2023, I began a jointly supervised PhD in Economics at Université Marie et Louis Pasteur (CRESE, Besançon) and the University of Sousse (LAMIDED). My dissertation, Corporate Social Responsibility: Between Information Asymmetries and Network Externalities, is supervised by Professors Lionel Thomas and Khaireddine Jebsi. It examines firms' incentives to adopt socially responsible practices across different market environments. My defence is scheduled for November 2026.

From 2024 to 2026, I was awarded the prestigious France Excellence Eiffel doctoral scholarship, a programme of the French Ministry for Europe and Foreign Affairs supporting outstanding international students and researchers. This fellowship supported the development of my doctoral research in France.

My research lies mainly at the intersection of industrial organization and theoretical microeconomics. I study corporate social responsibility, contract theory, asymmetric information, network externalities, imperfect competition, outsourcing and innovation incentives. My work has resulted in articles published in international peer-reviewed journals, conference presentations and ongoing research projects.

Alongside my research, I have developed teaching experience in microeconomics, econometrics and mathematics, first at the University of Sousse and later in Besançon. Since September 2026, I have held an ATER teaching and research appointment at Université Marie et Louis Pasteur. I also serve as a doctoral students' representative on the CRESE council.

De Smith aux algorithmes

Une histoire de la pensée économique · From Smith to Algorithms

I am currently writing a book that traces the development of economic thought, from the earliest formulations of political economy to today's questions about digital markets and artificial intelligence.

The project connects major schools of economic thought with their intellectual and historical contexts, methodological debates and institutional transformations.
