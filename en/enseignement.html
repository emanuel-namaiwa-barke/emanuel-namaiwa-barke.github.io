Microeconomics

Preferences, individual choice, allocation mechanisms and competition, from first to third undergraduate year.

Microeconomics 4 — Allocation Mechanisms and EquilibriaUniversité Marie et Louis Pasteur · Besançon · Third-year Economics · 2025–2027⌄

Outline based on course materials

  1. 01
    Introduction to theoretical microeconomics and allocation mechanisms

    Introduction to modelling, preferences, allocations, equilibria and efficiency criteria.

  2. 02
    Indivisible goods: allocation by power

    Examination of power relations, jungle equilibrium and serial dictatorship in the allocation of indivisible goods.

  3. 03
    Indivisible goods: voluntary exchange

    Analysis of decentralised exchange and equilibrium allocations when agents can trade voluntarily.

  4. 04
    Exchange economies and competitive markets

    Study of competitive equilibrium, endowments, relative prices and efficiency in an exchange economy.

  5. 05
    Exchange economies and carbon allowance markets

    Examination of tradable allowances as a way to regulate a good and their implications for exchange.

Microeconomics — Market StructuresUniversité Marie et Louis Pasteur · Besançon · Second-year AES · 2026–2027⌄

Outline based on course materials

  1. 01
    Introduction: concepts and market mechanisms

    Overview of supply, demand, prices, allocations and main market structures.

  2. 02
    Perfect competition

    Analysis of perfect-competition assumptions, market equilibrium and its stability.

  3. 03
    Monopoly

    Study of market power, pricing or output decisions and welfare implications.

  4. 04
    Oligopoly: Cournot duopoly

    Analysis of strategic interactions when firms compete by choosing quantities.

  5. 05
    Oligopoly: Bertrand duopoly

    Analysis of pricing strategies and equilibrium under price competition.

  6. 06
    Product differentiation and the Hotelling model

    Introduction to competition between differentiated firms using a spatial-location model.

Introduction to MicroeconomicsUniversité Marie et Louis Pasteur · Besançon · First-year Economics · 2026–2027⌄

Indicative thematic outline

  1. 01
    Fundamental principles of microeconomics

    Introduction to scarcity, rational choice, economic agents and market mechanisms.

  2. 02
    Supply, demand and market equilibrium

    Study of price determination and adjustments in quantities supplied and demanded.

  3. 03
    Preferences, utility and consumer choice

    Overview of preferences, budget constraints and utility maximisation.

  4. 04
    Production, costs and firm decisions

    Introduction to production functions, costs and profit maximisation.

  5. 05
    Competitive equilibrium and surplus

    Study of consumer and producer surplus and gains from trade.

MicroeconomicsUniversité de Sousse · FSEGS · First-year Economics and Management · 2023–2024⌄

Indicative thematic outline

  1. 01
    Introduction to microeconomic analysis

    Overview of scarcity, economic decisions and agents’ behaviour.

  2. 02
    Consumer theory

    Study of preferences, indifference curves and choice under budget constraints.

  3. 03
    Individual and market demand

    Examination of demand determinants and the effects of price and income changes.

  4. 04

Econometrics

Regression models, estimation and economic interpretation, from introductory to third-year methods.

EconometricsUniversité Marie et Louis Pasteur · Besançon · Third-year Economics and Management · 2025–2027⌄

Outline based on course materials

  1. 01
    Preliminaries — Matrix algebra review

    Review of matrix operations and their role in representing and estimating econometric models.

  2. 02
    Chapter 2 — Simple linear regression

    Introduction to the single-regressor model, its assumptions and parameter interpretation.

  3. 03
    Chapter 3 — Multiple linear regression

    Study of OLS estimation, estimator properties, statistical tests and model comparison.

Introduction to EconometricsUniversité de Sousse · FSEGS · Third-year Economics and Management · 2023–2024⌄

Indicative thematic outline

  1. 01
    Introduction to econometric methods

    Overview of econometric objectives, data types and model specification.

  2. 02
    Simple linear regression

    Analysis of the relationship between two variables using ordinary least squares.

  3. 03
    Multiple linear regression

    Introduction to estimation and interpretation of partial effects of multiple regressors.

  4. 04
    Hypothesis testing and goodness of fit

    Overview of common significance tests and measures of model fit.

Statistics

Statistical estimation and hypothesis testing for economic data analysis.

Inferential StatisticsUniversité de Sousse · FSEGS · Third-year Economics and Management · Semester 1 (2023) · 21 hours of tutorials⌄

Indicative thematic outline

  1. 01
    Sampling and sampling distributions

    Study of the variability of sample statistics and the central limit theorem.

  2. 02
    Point estimation and estimator properties

    Introduction to population parameter estimation and the concepts of bias, precision and consistency.

  3. 03
    Confidence interval estimation

    Construction and interpretation of confidence intervals for means, proportions and variances.

  4. 04
    Statistical hypothesis testing

    Analysis of Type I and Type II errors and standard tests for means, proportions and variances.

Mathematics

Foundations of mathematical analysis used in economic reasoning.

Indicative thematic outline

  1. 01
    Real-valued functions

    Study of domains, limits and continuity of functions.

  2. 02
    Differentiation and function analysis

    Introduction to differentiation rules, monotonicity and extrema.

  3. 03
    Functions commonly used in economics

    Study of polynomial, exponential and logarithmic functions in economic analysis.

  4. 04
    Functions of several variables

    Introduction to partial derivatives and multivariate economic functions.

  5. 05
    Elementary optimisation

    Introduction to unconstrained optimisation and basic constrained economic problems.
